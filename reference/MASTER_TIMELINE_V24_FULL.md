# 6. UofT MASc → MIT EECS PhD — RESEARCH OUTPUT & PROBABILITY MODEL

MIT EECS PhD admission is gated by research output, faculty fit, and connections — not credentials. Honest tiers:

**Tier 1 — No connections (5–10%/cycle):** 2 first-author publications (≥1 at IEEE/USENIX workshop), thesis with legible novel contribution, cold-email 5–8 MIT faculty with your best paper → one positive reply before applying, specific letters. Backup: Waterloo/UofT/McGill PhD.

**Tier 2 — Moderate connections (15–30%/cycle):** supervisor personally knows MIT faculty, or you attended an MIT workshop/summer school and impressed someone, or co-authored with someone in MIT's network; preliminary positive contact before applying. A direct "look at my student" email from your supervisor beats 10 cold applications.

**Tier 3 — Substantial connections (35–55%/cycle):** co-authorship with MIT faculty (visiting semester or remote collaboration), or MIT visiting-researcher status during MASc, or a potential advisor has verbally invited your application; 3+ publications including a top-4 venue.

**Realistic sequence:** P22–P24 produce T3/T4 paper from thesis + email MIT faculty. P24–P26 explore visiting-researcher; second paper; supervisor recommendation to target faculty. P26+ apply to MIT + Canadian PhDs in parallel; if rejected, do a Canadian PhD, publish, and reach MIT as a postdoc (relationship-driven, not a lottery). **Career outcome is identical via the Canadian-PhD → MIT-postdoc path — MIT is prestige, not prerequisite.**

# 12. FEASIBILITY MAP, BOTTLENECKS & CONTINGENCY

## Load-bearing assumptions & failure responses
| Assumption | Holds? | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Canadian + 1 online concurrently, sustained | Moderate-High | extend timeline 1–3 yrs; go sequential |
| MASc produces MIT-competitive output | Moderate | Canadian PhD → same career outcome |
| MIT EECS PhD admission | Low (5–55% by tier) | Canadian PhD → MIT postdoc |
| Stanford JD/MD in 40s | Very Low (~2% MD) | Canadian JD/health-law LLM; skip MD |
| Physical domains sustainable 40–52 | Moderate | scale to maintenance; drop highest-risk |

## The four real bottlenecks
1. **2028–2032 compression (highest risk):** BIT + 2 WGU MS + SANS BACS, then EET + MEng + SANS MSISE. Fix: slow the self-paced WGU work into gaps; never run EET-day + MEng-eve at simultaneous peak; budget 1–3 yrs slippage.
2. **MASc research vs MCAT (age 32–34):** publications and MCAT both need deep focus. Fix: pick one for the MASc window; if MIT is the goal, publications win and MCAT moves into the PhD.
3. **Age-30 tactical launch vs peak academic load:** stagger the tactical domains across 30–35, not all at once.
4. **Physical aging 40–52:** front-load high-impact domains to 30–40; maintenance after; de-prioritize BASE/HALO.

## Contingency branches
- **MIT rejected (95% base rate):** strongest Canadian PhD → publish → MIT postdoc. Career impact ≈ zero.
- **Timeline slips 2–3 yrs (likely):** absorb it; nothing is date-critical after the diploma. Never trade depth for a date.
- **Stanford JD/MD doesn't happen (likely for MD):** Canadian JD or health-law LLM serves the governance goal; MD not strictly required for the Harvard health-law publications.
- **Burnout/health event:** fall to the Minimum Viable Version (below).
- **D-Central takes off (best failure mode):** pivot from credential accumulation to operational leadership — the mission was always the point.

## Minimum Viable Version (protect this above all)
CST Networking → CST Security → OSCP→OSCE3 → WGU BSNES → one strong master's → Carleton BIT → UofT MASc → a research PhD (MIT or Canadian) → D-Central built → 20–30 publications. This alone is a top-fraction-of-a-percent career. Everything beyond is upside, not requirement.
