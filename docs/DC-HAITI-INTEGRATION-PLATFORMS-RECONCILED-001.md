# DC-HAITI-INTEGRATION-PLATFORMS-RECONCILED-001 — Haiti Integration Platforms (Drone Zoe / D-Central Ecosystem), Consolidated (v1, generated 2026-09-05)

Per [DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001](../standards/DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001.md), topic `haiti-integration-platforms` (12 docs).

## Current understanding

Twelve documents from the "Drone Zoe" and "VDI Solutions" projects describing a multi-sector, drone-
centered development and credentialing platform for Haiti. As with several other Haiti-related topics in
this pipeline, this topic contains multiple near-duplicate document clusters, one of which is already
flagged by the pipeline's own Stage 4 dedup metadata as a genuine unresolved conflict (not something this
Consolidator pass resolves — see Unresolved Tensions).

**Enterprise & Partnerships for Haiti's Unified Development Framework (incorporated):** the strategic
partnership layer for the broader multi-sector network — white-label solutions, government partnerships,
research partnerships, private-sector partnerships, a white-label implementation process, a governance/
IP framework, enterprise-partner benefits, implementation case studies, and partnership onboarding
guidance [§1-9]. Frames the network's core commitment as maintaining "democratic, community-centered
principles" even as external institutions engage with it commercially.

**Drone Zoe Haiti training/credentialing documents (Decentralized Open Credentialing System and
micro-credential-system, both incorporated as one claim set):** the Decentralized Open Credentialing
System document defines the overall training ecosystem — vision/guiding principles, integration with the
broader Drone Zoe ecosystem, a blockchain-based verification system, a stackable micro-credential
framework, a mobile learning platform, three named skill tracks (Technical, 90 hours; Infrastructure, 55
hours; Operations, 75 hours), and several economic-participation pathways (home-based infrastructure,
service-based earning, knowledge monetization, and named user pathways for apartment dwellers, technical
enthusiasts, community entrepreneurs, and regional developers), closing with blockchain-credential
implementation, learning-management implementation, marketplace integration, decentralized training hubs,
and a knowledge-commons/sustainable-economic-model section. The shorter micro-credential-system document
covers the same "Stackable Certification Structure" (individual technical skill badges building toward
stackable certifications) that the larger document's own micro-credential-framework section describes —
its content is a component or excerpt of the same credentialing design rather than an independently-scoped
system.

**Comprehensive Democratized Development Framework for Haiti and Multi-Sector Development (both
incorporated, substantial thematic overlap noted):** together these describe a broader multi-sector
development architecture beyond drones specifically — a core system architecture, sector-specific
implementations, sector-specific challenges and mitigation strategies (the telecommunications section
details last-mile connectivity, unreliable electricity, and technical-expertise gaps, mitigated via mesh
networking, integrated solar power, tiered training, and voice-first interface design), an integrated
financial architecture, a democratic governance framework, an integrated implementation roadmap, case
studies in integrated community development, financial sustainability and scaling, technology
infrastructure requirements, a partnership ecosystem, policy environment and advocacy, monitoring/
evaluation/learning, a risk-management framework, and — in the Multi-Sector Development document
specifically — an extended long-term-vision/impact section (environmental transformation, renewable
energy transition), security and critical-infrastructure integration, expanded sectoral integration,
unified platform technology, and a community-hub implementation guide. The two documents' content picks
up and continues each other's numbered sections (the Comprehensive Framework document's extraction begins
mid-document at its own §7, and the Multi-Sector Development document's extraction begins at "§9. Long-
Term Vision and Impact (continued)"), suggesting they are two extracted portions of a single larger
authored work rather than fully independent specifications — though neither carries an explicit
supersession or continuation note confirming this.

**Drone Zoe Haiti Complete Platform (incorporated):** the hardware/product-line specification — 10 drone
platforms, 8 sensor packages, and 30+ mission configurations, 95% 3D-printable for "complete technological
independence." Covers advanced data analytics/AI, on-demand drone service requests, a FLIGHTORY
integration ("Haiti Design Hub"), open-source sensor packages, an AI-powered drone ecosystem, cooperative
ownership model, training/certification (cross-referencing the credentialing documents above), and
Haiti-specific adaptations.

**Monetization strategy documents (Drone-Zoe-Haiti-Monetization-Strategy-Revenue-Streams,
expanded-monetization-guide, and haiti-drone-expanded-strategy, all incorporated as one claim set — a
third near-duplicate cluster within this topic):** all three describe the same 13-15-category revenue-
stream taxonomy for the Haiti Drone Cooperative at increasing levels of expansion — hardware/kits,
premium support/services, SaaS, training/certification, enterprise partnerships/licensing, hosting/
infrastructure, community/marketplace, crowdfunding/sponsorship, advertising/branding, freemium/premium
software features, IP/data licensing, financial services/insurance, and regional expansion/franchising —
each restating the same executive-summary framing ("outlines 15[+] revenue streams... while maintaining
technological sovereignty and community benefit"). `expanded-monetization-guide` and
`haiti-drone-expanded-strategy` both explicitly self-describe as expansions adding "exhaustive operational
details, implementation roadmaps, and tactical execution plans" beyond the base
Drone-Zoe-Haiti-Monetization-Strategy-Revenue-Streams document, rather than as independently-scoped
strategies. Also includes a distinct unified-cloud-platform/edge-to-cloud architecture section describing
a decentralized service marketplace (B2B edge-to-edge contracts, P2P insurance/risk pools, a decentralized
training/credential economy, impact bonds, data-as-a-service, tokenized asset leasing, distributed compute/
storage markets), an investment-philosophy/diaspora-connection section, and a 3-year revenue-mix roadmap
(Year 1: 40% SaaS/40% Services/20% Investment; Year 2: 50% SaaS/30% Services/15% Marketplace/5% DaaS;
Year 3: 40% SaaS/20% Services/20% Marketplace/10% DaaS/10% Tokenization).

**D-Central Ecosystem: Haiti Integration Framework (two disputed copies, both incorporated — see
Unresolved Tensions):** a decentralized-computing infrastructure framework built around a shared-
infrastructure model and mobile-first thin-client architecture, covering Haiti context analysis, multi-
stakeholder integration, a security/identity framework, NGO operations integration, an AI agent
architecture, a privacy-preserving tracking system, implementation strategy, technical specifications, an
economic model, cultural/practical adaptations, a deployment framework, risk management, success metrics,
and next-steps/conclusion. The longer of the two copies substantially expands the Multi-Stakeholder
Integration section (roughly 600 additional lines) and adds a "Core Architectural Principles" section near
its close that the shorter copy lacks.

**Haiti Security & Infrastructure Cooperative: Complete Operational Framework (incorporated):** the
security-focused operational counterpart to the Complete Platform document, specifying three primary
operations-center hub locations (Port-au-Prince, Cap-Haïtien, Les Cayes) each with armory-grade secure
hangar/storage, a drone maintenance clean-room workshop, a mission-control center with redundant
communications, and solar+generator-backed battery charging. Covers a modular micro-investment
architecture, an open-source technology foundation, expanded target markets and revenue streams, and
expanded personnel requirements.
