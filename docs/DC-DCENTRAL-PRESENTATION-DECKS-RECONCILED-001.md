# DC-DCENTRAL-PRESENTATION-DECKS-RECONCILED-001 — D-Central/CivicMesh Presentation Decks, Consolidated (v1, generated 2026-09-05)

Per [DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001](../standards/DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001.md), topic `dcentral-presentation-decks` (11 docs).

## Current understanding

Eleven presentation-deck specifications for D-Central Group / CivicMesh, spanning investor pitches, a
government sales pitch, and conference talks. Several document pairs are deliberately complementary
rather than duplicative — a content/narrative document paired with a separate production/design-brief
document for the same deck — and this consolidation treats each pairing as jointly covering one
presentation rather than as redundant content.

**Investor decks (INV-001, INV-002, INV-003, all incorporated):** a three-tier investor-outreach set.
INV-001 is the 15-slide Seed/Pre-Seed Pitch Deck for angel/impact investors, opening with the $38.7M
Ottawa enforcement-revenue gap (Ontario's November 2025 automated-speed-camera ban), introducing
TrafficMesh (mobile enforcement nodes with mandatory human officer review) as one of CivicMesh's five
modules, framing the cooperative structure as a structural moat competitors cannot replicate ("Flock
Safety cannot offer community revenue share without destroying their shareholder return model"), naming
a $7.5B/$500M+/$300M/$1.3B combined addressable market (Flock Safety/Nexar/ShotSpotter/Waze), a
$300K-$600K non-dilutive government-funding runway (IRAP/SR&ED/ISC/Mitacs) covering 70-85% of development
before investor capital, a $185K-$386K Year 1 / $2.5M-$5.3M Year 3 revenue projection, and a
$500K-$750K CAD seed ask at a $4M-$6M valuation cap. INV-002 is the 20-slide Deep Dive Investor Deck for
lead/institutional investors, structured across eight sections (problem/market, technical solution
architecture including the DID/VC credentialing system, legal/regulatory de-risking citing specific
compliance documents, traction, financial model, a detailed competitor matrix, team/partnerships, and the
ask) — explicitly built for due-diligence-stage scrutiny rather than a first pitch. INV-003 is a single-
page executive-summary one-pager for cold outreach, condensing the same problem/solution/traction/
funding/market/ask structure into one page with direct contact information.

**TrafficMesh government-sales decks (MKT-TM-GOV-002, PPTX-GOV-001, PPTX-GOV-002, all incorporated):**
MKT-TM-GOV-002 is the 12-slide narrative/speaker-notes script for pitching OC Transpo's CEO and VP
Operations, opening with the NYC MTA ACE precedent ($225M in 24 months, bus speeds +5%, collisions -20%),
positioning the mobile-enforcement approach as legally distinct from the banned fixed-camera category, a
citation-revenue split (OC Transpo 65% + a 5% OC Transpo reinvestment fund = 70% total to OC Transpo,
20% platform fee, 10% CCSC community fund — consistent with the 20% platform/MSSP share independently
stated in the TrafficMesh insurance-integration topic's FIN-TM-001 document), a small-and-specific pilot
ask (30 buses, 3 corridors, 6 months), and a privacy-architecture slide addressing human review, no
continuous recording, and public camera mapping. PPTX-GOV-001 is explicitly the production/design brief
for that same MKT-TM-GOV-002 narrative — not a duplicate but the paired document specifying color
palette, required data visualizations (a revenue-split pie chart, an installation-timeline Gantt chart),
required photos (real Ottawa test-deployment footage), and a "professional, not startup-flashy" design
principle appropriate to a government-procurement audience. PPTX-GOV-002 is a separate 15-slide City of
Ottawa Council presentation design brief (its own narrative content is in a document titled
MKT-TM-GOV-003, referenced but not itself a member of this topic), targeting City Council/CAO/Department
Heads with per-department value slides (Public Works, OC Transpo, Ottawa Police, Urban Planning,
Emergency Management, Bylaw Services), a large-format $38.7M revenue-gap visual, and a "political
defence" objection-matrix slide.

**CivicMesh investor deep-dive design brief (PPTX-INV-002, incorporated):** the paired production/design
brief for the same 20-slide deep-dive content INV-002 outlines — explicitly framed as "not a sales deck,
a due diligence document in presentation format," specifying a single clean architecture-stack diagram
(node → ingestion → officer portal → citation → revenue → CCSC → patronage), a financial-model
presentation requirement to show bear/base/bull cases and named sensitivity variables (citation volume,
officer review rate, opt-in rate) rather than headline numbers alone, and a requirement that regulatory
claims cite specific document references (e.g. "Legal opinion — REG-TM-001") rather than platitudes.

**Conference talks (PPTX-CONF-002, PPTX-CONF-003, both incorporated):** PPTX-CONF-002 is a 20-minute Smart
Cities-track conference talk structure targeting urban planners and transit authorities (ITS Canada, TRB
Annual Meeting, Smart Cities Connect, CUTA), framing city-fleet enforcement nodes as a byproduct pathway to
a municipal digital-twin asset and positioning the cooperative data model against smart-city vendor
lock-in. PPTX-CONF-003 is a 45-minute talk targeting the cooperative/policy community (Canadian Worker
Co-op Federation, Co-operatives and Mutuals Canada, Platform Cooperativism Consortium, New Economy
Coalition), framing D-Central as a case study answering the "platform economics extracts value from
communities" problem shared by Uber/Airbnb/Ring/Flock, using CivicMesh as its proof-of-concept and closing
with an audience discussion on scaling a cooperative technology ecosystem.

**MSSP partner-recruitment deck (PPTX-MSSP-001, incorporated):** a 10-slide design brief targeting
security integrators and IT managed-service providers, leading with the same $38.7M/444-municipality
revenue-gap framing used in the investor decks but pivoted to a partner-recruitment angle ("no certified
MSSP in the market yet"), specifying a clear MSSP unit-economics slide ($18-$88/node/month gross,
break-even at 100 nodes) and a "what D-Central provides vs. what the MSSP provides" split-column visual.

**Cooperative-economy thesis deck design brief (PPTX-INV-003, incorporated):** the design brief for a
12-slide deck targeting impact investors and cooperative capital funds, built around the stated thesis
"the mission and the moat are the same thing," including a dedicated "replication paradox" slide (the
same Flock Safety/Ring argument as INV-001's cooperative-moat slide) and an explicit note that cooperative
profit distribution at the operating-company level does not eliminate equity value at the holding-company
level — addressing a natural investor question about how a cooperative revenue model coexists with
conventional equity returns.

## Unresolved tensions

None identified. Every dollar figure that recurs across two or more of these eleven documents is
consistent — the $38.7M Ottawa enforcement-revenue-gap figure, the $7.5B/$500M+/$300M/$1.3B four-market
comparable sizing, the $300K-$600K non-dilutive government-funding range, and the citation-revenue-split
percentages all match wherever they are restated. Several document pairs (INV-002/PPTX-INV-002,
MKT-TM-GOV-002/PPTX-GOV-001) look at first glance like duplicate coverage of the same deck but are in
fact a deliberate content/design-brief split, confirmed by each pair's own explicit cross-reference to the
other half (e.g. PPTX-GOV-001: "Detailed slide-by-slide narrative is in MKT-TM-GOV-002. This document
provides the production brief for the PowerPoint designer").
