## Current understanding

Two companion catalogs with a deliberate division of scope: "Innovative Cooperative Business Models"
covers member-facing cooperative businesses (what communities could directly build and use);
"Cooperative Infrastructure" is explicitly framed as the "background businesses" that serve those
member-facing cooperatives (finance, professional services, technical infrastructure). Neither
overlaps the other's business categories, and the second explicitly positions itself relative to the
first ("As thousands of mesh-based businesses generate new revenue streams, they create massive
opportunities for cooperative infrastructure providers").

**Member-facing cooperative categories (incorporated, from Innovative Cooperative Business Models):**
data & intelligence cooperatives (community data trust, predictive analytics — $6.7M-8.5M/yr revenue
projections); resource-optimization cooperatives (space-time asset trading, skills/time banking);
innovation & creation cooperatives (distributed manufacturing, content production); sustainability/
circular-economy cooperatives (zero waste, renewable energy); and social-impact cooperatives (community
health, housing security) [§1-5]. A startup pathway (feasibility study → formation → pilot → scale →
sustainability) and five funding-source categories (member equity, cooperative loans, grants,
revenue-based financing, token presales) are given [§6]. Network-effects analysis models
inter-cooperative connection count growing combinatorially (2 co-ops = 1 connection; 100 co-ops = 4,950
connections) [§7]. A triple-bottom-line measurement framework (economic/social/environmental metrics)
closes the document [§8].

**Infrastructure/background cooperative categories (incorporated, from Cooperative Infrastructure):**
financial services (mesh-native cooperative banks with token-revenue-backed lending, decentralized
insurance, investment cooperatives — $20M/yr projected bank revenue vs. a stated $5M traditional
credit-union comparison); professional services (business consulting, legal, accounting cooperatives,
each claiming 2-3x higher per-professional earnings than traditional practice — e.g., "$400K per
consultant vs. $150K traditional"); technical infrastructure (equipment, data-center/edge-computing, and
green-energy cooperatives); market-making cooperatives (token exchange, logistics, marketing services);
and education/certification cooperatives (Mesh Economy University, quality-assurance certification)
[§1-5]. A three-phase implementation roadmap (Foundation months 1-6 → Expansion months 7-18 → Maturity
year 2+) projects $200M+ annual revenue at maturity [§6]. A combined Year-3 financial projection across
all 6 infrastructure sectors totals $159M revenue, 850 jobs, 110,000 members served, explicitly
compared against an estimated $500M captured by traditional (non-cooperative) equivalents with only 10%
local value retention [§7].
