# DC-MESH-COOPERATIVE-BUSINESS-MODEL-RECONCILED-001 — Mesh Cooperative Business Model Framework, Consolidated (v1, generated 2026-09-05)

Per [DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001](../standards/DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001.md), topic `mesh-cooperative-business-model-framework` (6 docs).

## Current understanding

Six documents spanning three distinct registers: two speculative catalogues of cooperative business
models enabled by mesh-network infrastructure, a tactical 90-day business-onboarding playbook, a
large-scale ($300M, Ottawa Black Chamber of Commerce) master implementation plan, and a two-part
conversation-extracted case study (DC-COOP-001 v1.0/v2.0) applying the cooperative model concretely to
one real business (Sod Boys Ottawa, a sod-laying company). The two DC-COOP-001 documents carry their
own explicit reconciliation notes (added during Stage 4 dedup review) clarifying a versioning claim that
would otherwise mislead readers — detailed in Unresolved Tensions below, though it is not actually an
unresolved conflict once read correctly.

**Innovative Cooperative Business Models for the Mesh Economy (incorporated):** a catalogue of ~10
speculative cooperative business categories enabled by mesh/token infrastructure — data & intelligence
cooperatives (a community data trust projecting $6.7M annual revenue with 80% member dividends; a
predictive-analytics cooperative projecting $8.5M/year), resource-optimization cooperatives
(space-time asset trading, skills/time banking), innovation cooperatives (distributed manufacturing,
shared content-production facilities), sustainability cooperatives (zero-waste processing, renewable
energy), and social-impact cooperatives (community health, community land trust housing) [§1-5]. Each
model includes a specific revenue breakdown and cost structure. A generic startup pathway (feasibility →
formation → pilot → scale → sustainability) and five funding-source categories (member equity,
cooperative loans, grants, revenue-based financing, token presales) [§6]. A network-effects section
modeling value multiplication as cooperative count grows, and triple-bottom-line (economic/social/
environmental) success metrics [§7-8].

**Mesh Network Business Onboarding Playbook (incorporated):** a tactical, day-by-day 90-day
implementation guide for a single business adopting mesh infrastructure — pre-launch assessment
checklists, a Day 1-7 "Foundation Week" hardware/software installation sequence, and week-by-week value-
capture targets escalating from $1,900 (Week 1) to $11,500 (Month 1) to $18,000 (Month 2) to $25,000+
(Month 3, $54,500 quarter total, $300,000+ annual projection) [full document]. Names specific revenue
streams by activation timeline (bandwidth resale and payment processing immediately; managed services
and B2B platforms by Month 2; white-label products and regional expansion by Month 3+), common
implementation challenges (staff resistance, technical issues, customer concerns) with paired solutions,
and 90-day success metrics (>70% cost reduction, >400% ROI, >90% customer satisfaction).

**Cooperative Infrastructure for Mesh Economy (incorporated):** a catalogue of "background business"
cooperative infrastructure serving the mesh economy itself, structured in the same category style as the
first document but at the infrastructure-provider layer — financial services (a mesh-native cooperative
bank projecting $20M/year across four novel loan products; decentralized insurance; investment
cooperatives), professional services (consulting, legal, and accounting cooperatives, each claiming per-
practitioner earnings 2.5-4x the traditional-industry average), infrastructure/technical cooperatives
(equipment, data-center, green-energy cooperatives), market-making cooperatives (token exchange,
logistics, marketing services), and education/certification cooperatives [§1-5]. A 3-phase (Foundation/
Expansion/Maturity) implementation roadmap and a combined financial projection totaling $159M revenue,
850 jobs, and 110,000 members served across all sectors by Year 3, explicitly contrasted against an
estimated $500M captured by traditional corporations serving the same population with only 10% local
value retention [§6-7].

**D-Central x OBCC Complete Master Implementation Plan (incorporated):** the largest-scope document — a
$300M-over-5-years plan to build Black community-owned mesh infrastructure in Ottawa via partnership with
the Ottawa Black Chamber of Commerce, projecting $1B+ community value and 10,000+ jobs created [Executive
Vision]. A 3-tier hardware architecture (Tier-3 gateway nodes at $5,000-10,000, Tier-2 community routers
at $400-1,000, Tier-1 access devices at $50-200) and platform services layer (DID/VC identity, IPFS
storage, encrypted communications, token/smart-contract transactions) [§2]. Four integrated sector
opportunities (communications, healthcare access, security services, resource management), a revenue-
model matrix and $DCENT token economy, and 5-year financial projections [§3-4]. A tiered micro-enterprise
framework from $0-500 entry-level opportunities up to $5,000-50,000 advanced business models [§5], large-
enterprise partnership strategies (telecom, healthcare, retail, financial services) [§6], and a
marketplace ecosystem section [§7]. This document is the most explicitly ambitious/promotional in tone
of the six and shares no sourced connection to the DC-COOP-001 case study beyond the shared
"cooperative infrastructure" concept — no cross-reference between the two exists in either document.

**DC-COOP-001 v1.0, Sod Boys Full Rebuild (incorporated):** a concrete, ten-tier cooperative
transformation blueprint applying the mesh-cooperative concept to one real Ottawa sod-laying business,
extracted from a conversation (not uploaded to a Project KB) [source metadata]. Restructures the company
from sole-owner "monarchy" into an Ontario worker cooperative under the Ontario Co-operative Corporations
Act, with four founding member classes (Founder Share with a 5-year veto sunset, Worker Member, Candidate
Member, Community Member) and a defined 5-year transition timeline to full cooperative governance [Tier
0]. Ten tiers covering identity infrastructure (D-ID sovereign member identity, credential registry),
economic architecture (a Lakou-Protocol-lite cooperative treasury, D-Credit internal economy, profit
sharing, equipment cooperative), governance (D-AGORA), operations (a named suite: D-MESH, D-MOVE,
D-ASSET, D-JOB, D-SUPPLY, D-CLIENT, D-CRED, D-QUOTE, D-ROUTE, D-TRAIN, D-WEATHER), client-facing systems,
supply-chain architecture, data sovereignty, community/stewardship, network growth (D-FRANCHISE,
D-MARKET), and technology stack, closing with a 5-phase implementation roadmap (Foundation through
Ecosystem Integration by Year 3-4) [Tiers 1-10, Implementation Roadmap].

**DC-COOP-001 v2.0, Sod Boys Full Expansion (incorporated):** an addendum to v1.0 from the same source
conversation, adding three new tiers without altering the original ten — Tier 11 D-SUSTAIN (a basic-needs
cooperative layer covering food, water, clothing, tools, and transportation as shared infrastructure,
explicitly framed as compensation beyond the wage line), Tier 12 D-FLEET (drone aerial operations,
IoT machine-intelligence telemetry, an asset-utilization engine, equipment rental exchange, preventive
maintenance protocol), and Tier 13 D-GROW (inbound lead management, a neighborhood-network-effect
protocol, field lead generation, content engine, partner network, local SEO infrastructure) [Tiers
11-13]. Also expands Tier 4's D-TRAIN component (drone licensing, peer teaching, a seasonal "D-CAMP"
gathering) and Tier 9's D-FRANCHISE component, and updates the total system registry count from 20 to
38 named subsystems [Change Log, Tier 4 Expansion, Updated Registry].

## Unresolved tensions

**None as a genuine Consolidator-level conflict** — but one relationship required care and is worth
restating here rather than leaving implicit. DC-COOP-001 v2.0's own document header states "Supersedes:
DC-COOP-001 v1.0," which read in isolation would suggest v1.0 can be discarded. Both source documents
carry a `reconciliation_note` (added during this pipeline's Stage 4 dedup review, not authored by this
Consolidator pass) explaining that this "Supersedes" claim is inaccurate as a blanket statement: v2.0's
own change log states "Tiers 0-10 from v1.0 remain unchanged" and v2.0 only specifies Tiers 11-13 in
full. v1.0 is therefore the sole complete source for Tiers 0-10 and must not be treated as superseded or
removed — the two documents together, not v2.0 alone, constitute the complete DC-COOP-001 specification.
This consolidation treats both as fully incorporated for that reason. Per DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001 §0,
marking either document superseded remains DC-DEDUP-STD-001's authority, not this pass's — the note above
simply carries forward what Stage 4 already determined so a reader of this consolidated doc isn't misled
by v2.0's header claim.

Separately, no genuine numeric conflict exists between the two catalogue-style documents (Innovative
Cooperative Business Models and Cooperative Infrastructure for Mesh Economy) or the Master Implementation
Plan — each proposes a distinct, non-overlapping set of business categories with independently-scaled,
illustrative revenue projections rather than competing estimates for the same entity, so none of the
dollar figures across these three documents are directly comparable or contradictory.
