# DC-MESH-FOOD-ECONOMY-RECONCILED-001 — Mesh Food Economy Business Models, Consolidated (v1, generated 2026-09-05)

Per [DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001](../standards/DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001.md), topic `mesh-food-economy-business-models` (4 docs).

## Current understanding

Three tightly-linked food-mesh economics documents (a community funding model, a restaurant cost/
revenue transformation analysis, and a food-industry-wide template applying that model across six food
business types) plus one section of a much broader cross-sector business catalog. All four are
mutually consistent on the figures they share.

**Community Funding Model (incorporated):** a $500,000 raise for a Local Food Mesh Network, structured
across five stakeholder tiers (Anchor Businesses 20%/$100K, Small Businesses 30%/$150K, Micro-
Enterprises 20%/$100K, Community Members 20%/$100K, Service Providers 10%/$50K), each with named
sub-categories (10 restaurants at $10K avg, 50 food trucks at $2K avg, 20 small farms at $2.5K avg, 200
delivery drivers at $250 avg, 75 home food businesses at $500 avg, 500 households at $200 avg, 5 tech
integrators at $10K avg) and multiple investment-structure options per tier (cash, equipment
contribution, revenue share, monthly installments, crop shares, time banking) [§1-6]. A four-tier
individual-investor benefits ladder (Bronze $50-100 through Platinum $501+) [§5]. Three creative
funding mechanisms — a multi-class cooperative (40/30/20/10% voting power split business/community/
worker/service-provider), revenue-based financing (repay 5-10% of savings until 1.5x returned), and
3-year 8% community bonds [§7]. A phased 16-week raise timeline, milestone-gated fund release (25% at
signing/equipment/launch/100-nodes), and a governance board with defined approval thresholds (60%
capex >$10K, 70% expansion, 80% fundamental changes) [§8-9]. Network-level financial projections
(Year 1 revenue $600K/net $300K, Year 3 revenue $3.6M/net $2.6M) and three named case examples (a food
truck collective, a farm cooperative at 880% ROI, a 50-family investment club) [§10-11].

**Restaurant Mesh Transformation (incorporated):** a line-item current-cost vs. mesh-transformed-cost
analysis across ten operational categories (internet/phone, security, POS/payment processing, delivery,
energy, marketing, inventory, utilities, cloud-to-edge computing, customer acquisition) for a single
restaurant, each with a specific before/after monthly figure and named mechanism (e.g., bandwidth
resale turning a $950-1,800/month internet cost into a $2,500/month revenue position; blockchain
payment processing cutting $2,400/month to $409/month) [§1-10]. Aggregates to $259,200/year current
cost vs. $29,388/year net cost after transformation — an 88.7% cost reduction plus $42-84K in new
revenue, for a total annual benefit of $271,812-313,812 (385-445% ROI, 4-6 month payback) [Comprehensive
Financial Impact]. Its own worked example (Maria's Mexican Restaurant, $10,000 investment, $80,892
Year-1 benefit) is the same example the Funding Model document cites for its Anchor Business tier,
confirming the two documents share one consistent underlying model.

**Local Food Mesh Template (incorporated):** generalizes the restaurant transformation model across six
food-sector business types — grocery stores ($360K→$160,800/yr, plus new revenue), farms ($81.6-87.6K→
$13,800/yr), food trucks ($45.6K→$20,400/yr), food processors ($192K→$39,600/yr), farmers markets
($39.6K→$9,000/yr), and community kitchens/incubators ($84K→$27,600/yr) — each with sector-specific
mesh mechanisms (direct farm purchasing, IoT crop monitoring, dynamic truck positioning, edge SCADA,
unified vendor platforms, predictive equipment maintenance) [§2]. A cross-industry supply-chain
analysis claims mesh disintermediation cuts markup from 35-50% to 10-15% and settlement time from
30-90 days to 24 hours via smart contracts [§3]. A three-phase rollout explicitly matches the Funding
Model document's own numbers — **Phase 1 (months 1-3): 10 restaurants, 2 grocery stores, 5 farms, 1
processor, 20 food trucks, requiring the identical $500,000 investment** — scaling to 500+ businesses
by Phase 3 (months 10-18) [§5]. Business-size-tiered financial projections (small $40-70K/yr benefit,
medium $200-400K/yr, large $700K-1.5M/yr) and regional network projections (500-business network:
$75-125M annual savings, 500-1,000 jobs) [§6].

**D Central Comprehensive Sector & Business Integration Matrix, Food & Agriculture section (incorporated,
partial):** this 20-sector catalog's Food & Agriculture section lists three primary businesses (IoT
Greenhouse Systems $12K, Mobile Soil Testing $8K, Farm Data Cooperative $20K) and supporting agtech/
supply-chain/market businesses [§8, Food & Agriculture]. This is a much coarser, catalog-style entry
than the other three documents' detailed food-mesh economics — it names food-sector opportunities
without the line-item cost/revenue modeling the other three provide, and uses a different core-platform
vocabulary ($DCT token, MaaS/IDaaS/Data Co-op naming) than the other three docs' unbranded "mesh
network" framing. The rest of this document (all 19 other sectors, cross-sector synergies, and platform-
wide revenue/metrics sections) is out of scope for this topic and is covered by the sibling topic
`dcentral-obcc-sector-implementation`.

## Unresolved tensions

None identified in this pass among the three core food-mesh documents — the Funding Model's $500,000
Phase-1 raise, the Restaurant Mesh Transformation's per-category savings figures, and the Local Food
Mesh Template's phased rollout all use consistent, cross-referencing numbers (the identical $500,000
figure, the identical Maria's Restaurant example). The fourth document (dcentral-sectors-comprehensive.md)
uses a different platform-branding vocabulary ($DCT, MaaS/IDaaS) than the other three's generic "mesh"
framing for what appears to be the same underlying infrastructure — noted as a terminology
inconsistency rather than a substantive conflict, since neither approach makes a claim the other
contradicts.
