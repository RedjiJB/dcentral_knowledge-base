## Current understanding

Three documents for the same product from different angles: Master-Platform is the full product/
business spec; marketing-site is the HTML landing page; Game-Suite is the detailed per-game design
document. All three are consistent on every checkable figure (pricing, game count, game names).

**Product spec (incorporated):** a game-based CompTIA A+ (220-1101/220-1102) certification prep
platform — ten integrated games, a learning-management dashboard with AI-driven knowledge-gap analysis,
a practice-exam simulator, and a mobile companion app [§Product Components]. Three subscription tiers:
Starter ($29.99/mo, 3 games), Professional ($49.99/mo, all 10 games + full analytics), Premium ($149
one-time, 6 months + exam voucher rebate), plus an Enterprise volume-licensing option [§Subscription
Packages]. A five-phase learning methodology (Assessment & Foundation → Domain Mastery → Integration &
Application → Exam Preparation → Certification Readiness) [§Learning Approach]. A pass guarantee (3
extra free months if the recommended study plan is completed and the exam is failed) and an exam-
voucher rebate up to $50 for Premium subscribers [§Guarantee & Certification Support]. Three customer
testimonials are quoted, including a claimed 32% pass-rate improvement at one technical college
[§Success Stories].

**Marketing landing page (incorporated):** a full HTML/CSS marketing site restating the same core
pitch, pricing tiers (identical $29.99/$49.99/$149 figures), and testimonials verbatim [throughout].
The page's games section displays only 8 of the 10 games as cards (omitting Mobile Device Master and
Tech Time Machine) — an incompleteness in this particular page's content, not a contradiction of the
product spec's stated 10-game roster, which the Game-Suite document confirms in full.

**Game-by-game design (incorporated):** full design detail for all 10 games named in the product spec
— Tech Trek Challenge (quiz-based, 5 difficulty levels), PC Builder Workshop (hardware/OS assembly
simulation across 5 named scenarios), Network Defense (networking/security strategy across 4 challenge
modes), IT Emergency Room (timed troubleshooting triage), Command Line Conquest (Windows/Linux CLI
challenges), Tech Term Titan (terminology mini-games with spaced repetition), Support Desk Simulator
(customer-service role-play with dialogue consequences), Security Response Team (incident-response
scenario decisions), Mobile Device Master (device configuration across 5 platform types), and Tech Time
Machine (technology-evolution timeline challenges) [§Game 1 through §Game 10]. A 3-phase implementation
strategy sequences the 10 games into Core (3 games) → Specialized (3 more) → Professional-Skills (final
4) rollout groups, and a suggested individual study strategy is given [§Implementation Strategy, §Study
Strategy].
