# DC-OBCC-SECTOR-RECONCILED-001 — D-Central x OBCC Sector Implementation, Consolidated (v1, generated 2026-09-05)

Per [DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001](../standards/DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001.md), topic `dcentral-obcc-sector-implementation` (2 docs).

## Current understanding

`dcentral-sectors-comprehensive` is an exhaustive 20-sector business-opportunity catalog;
`D-Central-x-OBCC-Complete-Master-Implementation-Plan` is a broader master plan covering platform
architecture, revenue models, and business tiers, which restates only a narrow 4-sector subset of the
first document's 20 sectors alongside its own financial/business framework.

**Sector catalog (incorporated):** 20 critical-infrastructure and cross-cutting sectors (Communications,
Healthcare, Emergency Services, Energy, Water, Transportation, Financial Services, Food & Agriculture,
Manufacturing, Chemical/Hazmat, Education, Retail, Tourism, Media, Real Estate, Waste Management, Legal,
Social Services, Sports/Recreation, Environmental Services), each broken into "primary businesses" (named
business models with specific capital-investment ranges, e.g., "Community ISP Cooperative,
$25,000-50,000") and "peripheral & support businesses" [§Critical Infrastructure Sectors, §Cross-Sector
Infrastructure Domains]. Core platform capabilities referenced throughout are MaaS (Mesh-as-a-Service),
IDaaS (DID/VC issuance), a Data Co-op, a **$DCT** token economy for micro-payments and DAO revenue
sharing, Edge AI, and IoT integration [§Overview]. Closing sections cover an integration-synergies
matrix, an implementation-prioritization framework, a revenue-model summary, success metrics, and risk
mitigation.

**Master implementation plan (incorporated):** a platform-wide mission/value-proposition statement and
mesh-network architecture overview [§1-2]; a narrower 4-sector "Integrated Sector Opportunities" section
(Communications Network, Healthcare Access Network, Security Services Network, Resource Management
Services) — a strict subset of the sector-catalog document's 20, not a competing or contradicting list
[§3]; a revenue-stream matrix and a **$DCENT**-named token economy with 5-year financial projections
[§4]; a three-tier micro-enterprise framework by investment size ($0-500 entry, $500-5,000 growth,
$5,000-50,000 advanced) [§5]; large-enterprise integration strategy across four named partner categories
(telecom, healthcare, retail, financial services) [§6]; and a marketplace-ecosystem section covering
current services and future expansion [§7].

## Unresolved tensions

**The platform token is named two different things across the two documents.** The sector catalog
names the platform's micro-payment/DAO-revenue token **$DCT** (referenced twice, including specific
governance/service-provision amount ranges). The master implementation plan names its token economy
section **$DCENT** with its own 5-year financial projections. Neither document cross-references the
other's token name, and nothing in either source indicates $DCT and $DCENT are the same token under two
names, a renaming over time, or genuinely two different tokens. Left as an open naming conflict rather
than assumed to be the same token, since the financial projections in each document would need to be
read against a single named asset to be meaningful together.
