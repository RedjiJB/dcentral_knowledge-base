## Current understanding

This topic is dominated by an extreme content-duplication pattern that must be understood before its
substance can be usefully summarized — see Unresolved Tensions for the full finding. In short: three of
the eleven member documents are the same ~5,000-line master document ("Complete Enhanced Open-Source
Cooperative Resilience Framework for Haiti") in three different formats/drafts, and six more of the
eleven documents are two different templated treatments of the same six crisis domains (Health, Food,
Inter-Sector Cascades, Climate, Government of Haiti Recommendations, International Community
Recommendations) that the master document already covers. Only one document (the Rezilyon Digital mesh-
network plan) is substantively independent. This consolidation describes the master framework's content
once, at chapter level, and describes the six-crisis-domain template pattern once rather than repeating it
across every document that restates it.

**Rezilyon Digital: Enhanced Haitian Community Mesh Network (incorporated):** an independent-
infrastructure plan combining a diaspora-skills-bridge program, blockchain-based community investment and
a token-economy currency-replacement strategy, elder-integrated democratic governance, and explicit
political-capture-resistance/community-defense measures against outside co-option [§1-5]. A 36-month
realistic development-and-funding timeline, a technology-obsolescence and integration strategy, NGO/
community-organization integration guidance, and an enhanced implementation roadmap [§6-9]. Later
sections add an NFT/microtransaction architecture, mobile-wallet/decentralized-identity integration,
partnership/funding strategy, success metrics, cultural-preservation-without-commodification guidance, a
fair diaspora-investment framework, and an AI-powered content-laboratory platform with its own economic
model and governance structure — the same infrastructure/blockchain/token/elder-governance/NGO-
partnership themes recur here as in the master framework's early chapters (see below), suggesting this
document and the master framework's opening sections share a common origin, though neither states this
explicitly.

**The master framework, in its three surviving copies (all incorporated as one claim set — see
Unresolved Tensions for why they are treated jointly): "Complete Enhanced Open-Source Cooperative
Resilience Framework for Haiti," "complete-enhanced-haiti-framework" (an expanded, numbered-section
variant subtitled "Comprehensive Implementation Guide with HCCC Integration"), and
"complete-merged-haiti-framework" (a LaTeX source rendition of the identical document).** Its core
innovation is the **Haitian Cooperative Coordination Center (HCCC)** — a unified cross-sector command
architecture eliminating security/health/education/food/governance/economic silos through real-time
joint planning cells, community-controlled multi-source intelligence fusion (HUMINT/OSINT/SIGINT/GEOINT),
and digital-twin crisis-simulation modeling [§1, §4]. Structured across roughly thirteen chapters: an
executive summary framing the transformation from "aid recipient to technology creator"; a Crisis Context
chapter analyzing UN Economic and Social Council Report E/2025/59 and cross-sector dependency mapping;
foundational cooperative/open-source/democratic-governance/economic-justice principles; the HCCC
technology architecture itself plus a federated edge-computing network and climate-integrated mesh
networking; an open personnel framework (a National Skills Acceleration Program and a National Cooperative
Credential Ledger); an open-source security framework built on federated security networks and a git-
based SOP repository; climate-integrated standard operating procedures; complete supply-chain sovereignty
under a four-tier localization model with cross-border cooperative-alliance procurement; sector-by-sector
implementation with cross-border integration; an enhanced financial-architecture chapter (cooperative
micro-equity pools, results-based financing linked to World Bank/IDB/EU blended-finance programs,
community micro-insurance, and an open aid ledger); a climate-resilient implementation timeline;
integrated performance metrics; climate-adaptive risk management; stakeholder engagement and governance;
digital-twin/crisis-simulation systems; global replication and knowledge transfer; and a long-term,
explicitly "millennial" (thousand-year) sustainability vision, closing with technical annexes.

**The six-crisis-domain sections (2.1.3 Health, 2.1.4 Food Security, 2.2.1 Inter-Sector Cascades, 2.2.2
Climate as Universal Crisis Multiplier, 2.3.1 Government of Haiti Recommendations, 2.3.2 International
Community Recommendations — all incorporated) and their expanded counterpart ("expanded-section-2"):**
each of the six numbered fragment documents applies one consistent template to its named crisis domain —
a "Cooperative-Owned Federated [Domain] Intelligence Layer," a "Cooperative [Domain] Economics Integration
Architecture," a "[Domain] Technological Sovereignty Framework," "Cultural [Domain] Preservation &
Integration," and "Environmental [Domain] Regeneration Integration," followed by domain-specific crisis
responses (e.g. Health's Insulin Crisis Cooperative Response, HIV Treatment Continuity Networks, Blood
Supply Cooperative Networks, Cholera Prevention Networks; Food Security's response to 5.5M people in acute
food insecurity and 289,000 malnourished children per the same UN report), a "Cooperative Caribbean
[Domain] Commonwealth" regional-federation structure, and a "Know Your [Domain] Cooperative" public
campaign. The "expanded-section-2" document applies a second, more technically detailed template to the
same six domains — an Overview, "[Domain] Crisis Context with Cooperative Analysis," a "Comprehensive
[Domain] Technical Architecture," an "Advanced GraphQL Federation Schema" for each domain, domain-specific
applications and solutions, a "Regional Caribbean [Domain] Federation Framework," public engagement,
a 24-month implementation timeline, success metrics, and a "Millennial [Domain] Vision" — largely restating
the master framework's own equivalent chapters (Crisis Context & UN Report Analysis; Universal Technology
Architecture with HCCC; sector-by-sector implementation; the millennial/thousand-year vision) at the
individual-domain level rather than adding domains the master framework omits.

## Unresolved tensions

**This topic is the most extreme content-duplication case found in this pipeline's Stage 6 pass, and it
is reported here as an observation for Stage 4 dedup review rather than resolved by this Consolidator
pass** (per DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001 §0, marking documents superseded or duplicate is DC-DEDUP-STD-001's
authority, not this skill's).

1. **Three documents are the same master document.** "Complete Enhanced Open-Source Cooperative
   Resilience Framework for Haiti" (unnumbered sections), "complete-enhanced-haiti-framework"
   (numbered §1-14, subtitled "Comprehensive Implementation Guide with HCCC Integration"), and
   "complete-merged-haiti-framework" (a LaTeX `\documentclass{article}` source file whose PDF metadata
   title is, verbatim, "Complete Enhanced Open-Source Cooperative Resilience Framework for Haiti") share
   an identical section sequence — Executive Summary → Crisis Context & UN Report Analysis →
   Foundational Principles → Universal Technology Architecture with HCCC → Enhanced Open Personnel
   Framework → (Climate-Integrated Open SOPs / Supply Chain Sovereignty) → Sector-by-Sector
   Implementation → Financial Architecture → Implementation Timeline → Performance Metrics → Risk
   Management → (Global Replication / Long-Term Vision) → Conclusion → Annexes — verified by comparing
   each document's own table of contents and section headers directly. These three are very likely three
   drafts or export formats of one authored document, not three independently-scoped specifications.
2. **Six documents restate the same six crisis domains the master framework already covers, using two
   different templates.** The 2.1.3/2.1.4/2.2.1/2.2.2/2.3.1/2.3.2 fragment set and the "expanded-
   section-2" document both cover exactly Health, Food Security, Inter-Sector Cascades, Climate,
   Government of Haiti Recommendations, and International Community Recommendations — the same six
   sub-areas the master framework's own "Crisis Context & UN Report Analysis" and "Sector-by-Sector
   Implementation" chapters address — each using its own template (a Commonwealth/Federation/Know-Your-
   Cooperative template for the first six; a GraphQL/Technical-Architecture/Millennial-Vision template
   for expanded-section-2) rather than adding a domain the master framework omits.
3. **The Rezilyon Digital mesh-network document shares substantial unstated thematic overlap with the
   master framework's own early chapters** (independent infrastructure, blockchain investment, token
   economy, elder governance, NGO integration, timeline) — this may be an earlier draft that the master
   framework's HCCC-centric architecture superseded and absorbed, but no document states this relationship
   explicitly, so it is reported as an observation rather than acted on.

Given this pattern, this consolidated document was written at chapter/section-template level rather than
claim-by-claim across all ~65,000 words of underlying source text — consistent with this pipeline's prior
practice for oversized topics (e.g. the provincial-security-network and opensecure-sector-use-case
consolidations), and specifically appropriate here because full claim-level extraction of three copies of
the same document plus two templated restatements of the same six sub-topics would multiply, not increase,
the actual information captured.
