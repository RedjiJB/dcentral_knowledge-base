## Current understanding

Two complementary FediFlow (Local Fediverse project) documents targeting higher-education customers:
one is the ROI/value-proposition case (why a university should buy), the other is the services catalog
(what they'd actually be buying to realize that ROI). Both are marketing/business-development documents
with sweeping figures, not independently verified outcomes.

**ROI and value-proposition claims (incorporated, stated as projections not verified results):**
quantified claims across five institutional domains — student recruitment (15-25% admission-yield
improvement, $2,000-5,000 cost-per-enrolled-student reduction), retention (3-7% retention improvement,
$500K-2M/yr "early warning system" value), alumni/fundraising (200-500% alumni engagement increase,
$1M-10M/yr major-gift pipeline increase), faculty research (300-800% research-visibility increase), and
operational efficiency ($200K-800K/yr marketing cost reduction) [§Quantifiable Academic ROI]. A 5-year
value-realization timeline projects Year-1 foundation building through Year-4/5 "market leadership,"
[§Implementation Timeline] culminating in a summary claiming 150-200% Year-1 ROI rising to 500-1000%+ by
Year 4-5 and $10M-50M+ cumulative 5-year value creation [§ROI Summary].

**Services catalog (incorporated):** three service tiers. Managed services cover strategic consulting
(social-media strategy, change management, crisis-communication planning), technical integration (LMS
integration — Canvas/Blackboard/Moodle — SIS synchronization, alumni-database integration), and
compliance/security (FERPA compliance management, research-data protection with IRB compliance,
international-student compliance) [§Comprehensive Academic Managed Services]. Content and community
services cover academic content strategy, research communication, and professional community moderation
distinguishing faculty/student/alumni/research communities as separate managed contexts [§Comprehensive
Academic Managed Services, Content & Community Services]. Specialized professional services and ongoing
consulting services round out the offering (not itemized further here; both sections consist of the same
style of named service line items as the managed-services section).

**How the two fit together:** the ROI document's claims (e.g., FERPA-related risk reduction, alumni
engagement gains, faculty research-visibility increases) map directly onto specific named services in
the catalog (FERPA Compliance Management; Alumni Engagement Strategy; Research Communication) — the
services catalog is the fulfillment mechanism for the ROI document's claims, not an independent or
overlapping pitch.
