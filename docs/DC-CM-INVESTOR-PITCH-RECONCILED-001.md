# DC-CM-INVESTOR-PITCH-RECONCILED-001 — CivicMesh Investor Pitch Decks, Consolidated (v1, generated 2026-09-05)

Per [DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001](../standards/DC-CONSOLIDATOR-STD-001.md), topic `civicmesh-investor-pitch-decks` (4 docs).

## Current understanding

Four investor/customer-facing pitch narratives, all built on the same underlying facts (the $38.7M
Ottawa ASE-ban revenue gap, the NYC MTA ACE precedent, the cooperative-model moat, and the government
funding stack) but aimed at different audiences: INV-001 is the 15-slide seed/pre-seed narrative for
angel investors, INV-002 is a 20-slide due-diligence deep dive for lead/institutional investors,
INV-003 is a one-page cold-outreach summary, and MKT-TM-GOV-002 is a 12-slide narrative aimed not at
investors but at OC Transpo's own leadership (the prospective pilot customer). All four are consistent
on every cross-checkable figure.

**INV-001 seed pitch (incorporated):** 15-slide narrative opening with the $38.7M Ottawa enforcement-
revenue gap, framing fixed cameras as politically (not technically) dead, introducing TrafficMesh as
mobile enforcement with mandatory officer review, citing the NYC MTA ACE precedent ($225M/24mo, +5%
bus speed, -20% collisions), positioning CivicMesh as a 5-module platform (TrafficMesh being one
vertical) with the cooperative structure as the structural moat competitors cannot replicate, sizing
the combined addressable market at ~$9.6B across four comparable categories (Flock $7.5B, Nexar $500M+,
ShotSpotter $300M, Waze $1.3B), noting government funding (IRAP/SR&ED/ISC/Mitacs) covers 70-85% of
build cost, current traction (OC Transpo pilot proposal in process, 5 HOA deployments, one insurance
conversation initiated, one Algonquin training partnership in discussion), financials ($185K-386K Year
1, $2.5M-5.3M Year 3, break-even at 150 managed nodes), a $500K-750K CAD seed ask (SAFE/convertible,
$4-6M cap), and a specific use-of-funds breakdown (2 engineers at $90K each, $100K Phase 2 hardware,
$40K legal, $50K marketing/BD, $30-130K working capital) [slides 1-15].

**INV-002 deep-dive deck (incorporated):** 20-slide institutional due-diligence version organized into
8 sections rather than a flat slide list: problem/market (adds 444-municipality provincial-variation
framing to the same $38.7M hook), solution architecture (goes to technical depth across all 5 CivicMesh
modules, node tiers, DID/VC credentialing, privacy architecture — depth INV-001 doesn't attempt),
legal/regulatory (summarizes REG-TM-001 HTA compliance and REG-CM-001 PIPEDA compliance, explicitly
scoping why the ASE ban doesn't reach TrafficMesh), traction/validation (same specific items as INV-001:
OC Transpo, HOA, funding, insurance, Mitacs), financial model (references FIN-CM-002 unit economics and
FIN-TM-001 phased revenue model, plus insurance-API revenue and an MSSP partner model INV-001 doesn't
mention), competitive analysis (a detailed matrix vs. the same four comparables), team/partners (adds
named research partnerships — Carleton, uOttawa, Algonquin — beyond INV-001's single-founder framing),
and the ask/use-of-funds (same seed ask structure, with Series A milestones added) [8 sections, 20
slides].

**INV-003 one-pager (incorporated):** single-page cold-outreach condensation carrying the identical
core facts as INV-001 — same $38.7M problem, same CivicMesh solution description, same traction items,
same $300K-600K non-dilutive funding range, same four-category market sizing, same $500K-750K seed ask
and SAFE/$4-6M-cap terms — with founder contact information appended, formatted as a single content
table rather than a slide sequence [§1 One-Pager Content].

**MKT-TM-GOV-002 OC Transpo customer pitch (incorporated):** a 12-slide narrative distinct in kind from
the other three — its audience is the prospective pilot customer (OC Transpo CEO/VP Operations), not an
investor, so the ask is a pilot agreement, not capital. Opens with the same NYC MTA ACE proof point,
then pivots to Ottawa-specific bus-lane enforcement gaps, frames the ASE ban as creating a legal
opening rather than a workaround (citing REG-TM-001 directly), gives a live officer-portal demo slide
using real Ottawa test-vehicle footage as "the most important slide," details a specific revenue-share
model (OC Transpo 65% of net citation revenue, 20% platform fee, 10% CCSC community fund, 5% OC Transpo
reinvestment fund) projecting $225K-900K in a first 6-month pilot, leads with privacy architecture
before objections can be raised, and proposes a small, specific 30-bus/3-corridor/6-month pilot scope
with a concrete installation timeline (garage access 02:00-06:00) [slides 1-12].
