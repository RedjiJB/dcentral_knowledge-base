# DC-GOV-002 — Conglomerate Governance & Capital Allocation Architecture

**Registry ID:** DC-GOV-002
**Status:** Design Complete — activates progressively with DC-VENTURE-001 phases
**Owner:** Company Zero
**Related:** DC-LIC-001, DC-VENTURE-001, DC-MOGUL-001, DC-LKB-001…003 (Lakou Protocol), Sovereign DAO OS, Cooperative Transparency Stack
**Superseded in part by:** DC-MOGUL-001 (reframes the principal from sole conglomerate owner to first principal and protocol author; the mechanics below remain in force)

---

## 1. Governing principle

DAOs do not replace managers; they replace the principal *as* manager. Every operating venture retains a human general manager. The governance layer's function is to make GMs accountable to transparent systems — open books, published metrics, vault-governed capital — rather than to the principal's daily attention. The structure is a family office implemented on D-Central rails: the principal governs, others operate.

## 2. Layer architecture

**Layer 1 — Company Zero (holding / family office).** Owns equity in every venture, holds all IP, owns the property company (Phase 4). The principal sits here as principal, not operator. The four-tier agent cabinet operates at this layer: reporting consolidation, document generation, compliance calendars, and financial roll-ups. The three refused departments remain refused — the cabinet advises and administers; it does not surveil or enforce.

**Layer 2 — Operating subsidiaries.** OpenSecure, the construction arm, the gym, the club: each a subsidiary with a hired GM compensated by profit-share or equity earn-in. GM hiring, not governance software, is what buys the principal out of day-to-day operations. Regulated subsidiaries follow the Regulated Entity Pattern (DC-LIC-001 §4) permanently: OpenSecure is never DAO-governed at the operational level because PSISA requires a designated accountable licence holder and inspectable conventional management.

**Layer 3 — DAO/co-op wrapper (community-facing ventures only).** The gym and social club operate as member-owned cooperative cells on Sovereign DAO OS tooling: members vote on programming, pricing tiers, and community reinvestment, while the GM executes and Company Zero holds real estate and brand. The Cooperative Transparency Stack provides open books to members — simultaneously the trust mechanism and the marketing differentiator.

**Layer 4 — Lakou Protocol (internal bank).** The vault system (DC-LKB-001…003) becomes the conglomerate's capital-allocation machinery. Each venture contributes to shared vaults on a published schedule: an operating reserve vault, an expansion vault that funds the next venture on the DC-VENTURE-001 sequence, a community/youth vault, and a member-benefit vault. Financing new ventures becomes a governed internal allocation process rather than a personal decision backed by personal guarantee. External capital — BDC, credit unions (structurally aligned with cooperatives), SR&ED and IRAP refunds — enters at the holding layer only.

## 3. Capital allocation process

Expansion-vault disbursements follow a fixed cycle: a venture proposal is prepared against a DC-VENTURE-001 phase trigger or a DC-MOGUL-001 template; the cabinet produces a standardized diligence package; the vault governance process votes; capital releases in milestone-gated stages mirroring the SR&ED trigger discipline of DC-TM-POC-001; repayment flows back into the vault as contributions from the funded venture. The vault maintains a loss reserve, and allocation decisions are made on portfolio logic across ventures rather than venture-by-venture optimism.

## 4. Principal time model at maturity

Weekly, two to three hours: cabinet-generated exception dashboard across all ventures. Monthly, one half-day: GM reviews, one per venture — numbers plus at most one decision each. Quarterly, two days: expansion-vault capital allocation and DAO assemblies for the member ventures. Annually: strategy, registry maintenance, and external capital relationships.

Non-delegable regardless of scale: PSISA designated licence-holder duties and any other statutory accountability (AGCO, WSIB), director and officer liabilities, primary banking relationships, and authorship of the vision and registry.

## 5. Honest sequencing constraint

This end-state architecture activates over years, not at launch. Phases 0–1 of DC-VENTURE-001 require the principal operating day-to-day on sites for roughly the first three to four years. The delegation ladder (DC-VENTURE-001 §6) is the only path to the time model above, and DAO tooling is layered onto a venture only after it is profitable and operationally boring. Governance automation cannot rescue a business with bad margins; it can only make a good business run without its founder.
