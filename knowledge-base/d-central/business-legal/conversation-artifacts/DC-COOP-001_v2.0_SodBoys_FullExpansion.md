## Tier 11: D-SUSTAIN — Basic Needs Cooperative Layer

*"A cooperative that only pays wages is a better employer. A cooperative that meets basic needs is a different kind of institution entirely."*

The sovereign cooperative does not stop at the job site boundary. D-SUSTAIN brings food, water, clothing, tools, and transportation inside the cooperative infrastructure — reducing crew cost of living, increasing real compensation beyond the wage line, and building the kind of loyalty that cash alone cannot buy.

Every system in this tier runs on D-Asset (for physical items), D-Credit (for the exchange layer), and D-Supply (for procurement). Nothing here requires new infrastructure — it rides the architecture already built.

---

### 11.1 D-FOOD: Cooperative Nutrition Program

Food is the highest-frequency basic need. An Ottawa field crew burns 3,000–4,000 calories doing physical outdoor work. D-FOOD treats crew nutrition as an operational input, not a personal problem.

**D-FOOD systems:**

**Cooperative bulk purchasing:**
- Weekly bulk food order aggregated across all crew (staples: rice, protein, snacks, beverages, produce)
- Partner with Ottawa food cooperatives (Just Food Farm CSA, Ottawa Organic, La Bottega for bulk)
- Crew opts into the weekly bundle — items charged at cost through D-Credit (below retail price)
- Surpluses redistributed to crew with highest need first (solidarity allocation)

**Pre-shift meal coordination:**
- Crew pre-orders breakfast or lunch through D-MESH the night before
- Orders aggregated and fulfilled by a rotating crew member on paid prep duty (earns D-Credits)
- Or: partnered with a local caterer for bulk morning orders delivered to the yard
- Crew pays at cost via D-Credit deduction; cooperative absorbs the coordination overhead
- Hot food at the yard before a 6am start is a retention tool no competing employer offers

**On-site hydration packs:**
- D-SUPPLY ensures every job site has a water/snack kit stocked and tracked (see D-WATER, 11.2)
- Energy snacks (granola bars, electrolyte drinks) ordered in bulk through D-SUPPLY, distributed through D-JOB pre-roll kit

**Seasonal food fund:**
- Off-season (November–March) the cooperative contributes to a food fund for returning members
- Funded at Seasonal Assembly vote from the cooperative profit pool
- Members who return the following spring receive the fund as a welcome-back credit

**Partner network:**
- D-CREDIT redeemable at partner food establishments near job sites and the yard
- Cooperative develops preferred vendor relationships with local Ottawa restaurants and delis
- Crew gets a "cooperative lunch discount" at partner establishments — negotiated as a block relationship

### 11.2 D-WATER: Site Hydration & Water Infrastructure

Ottawa summers reach 35°C with humidity. Water on site is not optional — it is a safety requirement and a productivity driver. D-WATER makes it systematic.

**Physical infrastructure:**
- Portable water stations (20L coolers) registered as D-ASSET with unique IDs
- Each station assigned to a job site in the D-JOB pre-roll gate
- Water station is a required pre-roll checklist item — crew cannot be dispatched to a site without confirmed water availability
- Station refilled at yard and tracked per D-ASSET check-out (weight sensor or manual log)

**Site water access:**
- D-RECORD stores site water access info per property: outdoor tap accessible Y/N, hose bib location, access code if gated
- Crew do not have to ask the client or figure it out on arrival

**Hydration monitoring:**
- Crew log water breaks in D-MESH (30-second tap: "Water break — 10 min")
- D-WEATHER triggers mandatory hydration alerts when heat index exceeds 32°C
- Water break compliance visible to foreman — not punitive, protective
- Crew who log consistent hydration compliance earn D-Credits (builds the habit)

**Water quality tracking:**
- Ottawa municipal water quality reports integrated (public data, no API cost)
- Flags any active boil advisories for sites in affected zones
- If a site has well water, D-RECORD notes whether it is potable

### 11.3 D-WEAR: Cooperative PPE & Clothing Program

**Safety equipment library (D-ASSET extension):**
Every piece of shared PPE is a registered asset:
- Hard hats (class-rated)
- High-visibility vests (class 2 minimum)
- Safety glasses
- Cut-resistant gloves (multiple sizes)
- Steel-toe boot vouchers (D-Credit redemption toward purchase)
- Rain gear (bibs, jackets)
- Sun protection (hats, neck covers, SPF — bulk purchased)

**Check-out/check-in:**
Same QR/NFC system as machines. Crew checks out PPE at the yard at shift start, checks in at end of day. Shared items tracked for condition. Any item graded "below standard" by crew is flagged for replacement.

**PPE inspection cycle:**
- Hard hats: visual inspection quarterly (cracks, degradation)
- Gloves: replaced when grip layer worn through (crew flags in D-MESH)
- Hi-vis: replaced annually or when reflectivity degrades
- Replacement triggers automatic D-SUPPLY reorder

**Personal PPE allowance:**
- Every worker member receives a seasonal PPE credit (D-Credits) to purchase their own personal safety items through the cooperative bulk catalog
- Candidate members receive a starter kit at onboarding (cost offset against first season D-Credit earnings)

**Cooperative uniform program:**
- Branded work wear (T-shirts, hoodies, ball caps) purchased in bulk through D-SUPPLY
- Crew pays cooperative cost (not retail) through D-Credit deduction
- Uniform tracking: who has what, condition, replacement schedule
- New client sites: uniform appearance is a professionalism and marketing signal

**Boot program:**
- Heavy-duty steel-toe work boots are the single highest individual PPE cost
- D-WEAR manages a cooperative boot fund: crew contributes small D-Credit amount weekly, fund covers replacement boots when needed
- Bulk purchase relationship with a supplier (Mark's, King's, or local boot shop) for cooperative pricing

### 11.4 D-TOOLS: Cooperative Tool Library

**Philosophy:** Tools should be available to whoever needs them, maintained by the collective, and tracked so nothing disappears.

**Tool categories in D-ASSET:**

| Category | Examples | Management |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Hand tools | Shovels, rakes, edgers, hand tampers | Common pool, yard check-out |
| Power tools | String trimmers, leaf blowers, hedge trimmers | Asset registry, charge/fuel tracked |
| Measuring & survey | Measuring wheels, levels, grade stakes | Pre-job kit, D-JOB assignment |
| Specialty tools | Sod cutters, core aerators, dethatchers | Heavy rotation, maintenance schedule |
| Safety tools | First aid kits, fire extinguishers | Required per site, D-JOB gate |
| Drone accessories | Batteries, cases, props, controllers | D-DRONE registry (Tier 12) |

**Personal tool incentive:**
Crew members who bring their own qualified tools (maintained, safe, appropriate) earn D-Credits per shift where the tool is used on a cooperative job. This rewards investment in personal tools and expands the cooperative's effective tool pool without capital expenditure.

**Tool maintenance rotation:**
- Weekly tool inspection logged by yard coordinator (or rotating crew member on D-Credit duty)
- Cleaning protocol after each job (mud, sap, concrete — specific notes per tool type)
- Sharpening schedule: blades, edgers, pruning tools logged and serviced
- Tool damage reported immediately in D-MESH — no shame, just data

**D-TOOLS cooperative purchasing:**
- Crew can propose new tool purchases through D-AGORA
- Tools valued under threshold: Operations Circle approval
- Tools above threshold: full member vote
- D-Credits can be pooled toward a crew-proposed tool purchase

**Tool sharing with D-FRANCHISE network:**
Specialty tools with low utilization (e.g., a dethatching machine used 8 days a season) are listed on D-MARKET for rental to network cooperatives. Earns rental revenue from tools that would otherwise sit idle.

### 11.5 D-TRANSIT: Cooperative Transportation Infrastructure

D-MOVE (Tier 4) handles job-site carpool chains. D-TRANSIT is the broader transportation layer — how the cooperative supports crew mobility beyond the work context.

**Cooperative vehicle pool:**
- Cooperative owns or leases a work van (or cargo van) as a shared asset
- Van registered in D-ASSET, available for crew booking outside work hours for cooperative business and approved personal use
- Booking through D-MESH (calendar picker, first-come cooperative-members-first)
- Usage logged (km, fuel), costs split to the using member's account

**OC Transpo partnership:**
- Cooperative negotiates employer group pass with OC Transpo (available under Ottawa Business Transit program)
- Passes subsidized through cooperative operating costs
- Crew without vehicles receive transit credit through D-Credit
- Reduces parking pressure at the yard, reduces carbon footprint

**Electric vehicle transition:**
- When the cooperative's vehicle(s) need replacement: EV van as cooperative policy
- EV charging station at the yard (Level 2 charger, registered D-ASSET)
- Night charging at cooperative rates
- As fleet electrifies, fuel costs trend toward zero

**Bicycle cooperative:**
- For crew living close to the yard or to job sites: cooperative bikes available for commute or short-distance crew transit
- Bikes registered in D-ASSET, maintained by crew on D-Credit maintenance duty
- E-bikes added as budget permits (Ottawa is increasingly e-bike-friendly)

**Winter transit support:**
- November–March: Ottawa winters make cycling and outdoor waiting difficult
- Cooperative allocates a transit support pool for off-season returning members
- Covers OC Transpo passes, rideshare credits, or additional income support during the slow period

## Tier 12: D-FLEET — Advanced Asset Architecture

*"Every machine has a utilization rate. Every idle hour is revenue that didn't happen. Every maintenance miss is an asset that dies early. D-FLEET makes this visible and actionable."*

D-FLEET transforms the equipment relationship from "machines we own" to "a productive capital network we manage." Drones extend the fleet into the air. IoT makes every machine a data source. The utilization engine optimizes allocation. The rental layer monetizes idle capacity. Maintenance becomes preventive rather than reactive.

---

### 12.1 D-DRONE: Aerial Operations Platform

Drones are not a luxury for a landscaping company — they are a productivity, quality, and marketing infrastructure that pays for itself in the first season.

**Operational uses:**

| Use Case | Value Delivered |
|---------|----------------|
| Pre-job site survey | Precise area measurement → accurate material quote → no shortfall or waste |
| Drainage mapping | Identify low points, slopes, problem areas before sod is installed |
| Progress documentation | Time-lapse of full install → premium client deliverable |
| Quality inspection | Check large sites from above for missed patches, poor seams, edge issues |
| Property record | Aerial imagery in D-RECORD for every client property |
| Marketing content | Drone footage of completed jobs → social media, website gallery |
| Competitor intelligence | Fly public roads, observe competitor activity (legal, airspace permitting) |
| Damage documentation | After-storm assessment for client properties |

**Transport Canada RPAS Compliance:**

All drone operations in Canada require registration and certification.

| Certificate | Scope | Requirement |
|------------|-------|------------|
| Basic RPAS Pilot | VLOS, uncontrolled airspace, not over people | Online exam (Transport Canada) |
| Advanced RPAS Pilot | All other operations incl. controlled airspace | Written exam + flight review |
| Drone registration | All drones 250g–25kg | $5/drone, Transport Canada portal |

Ottawa airspace notes:
- Macdonald-Cartier International Airport (YOW) creates controlled airspace over much of central/south Ottawa
- NOTAM checking required before any job site flight
- Advanced certificate required for most Ottawa metro sites
- D-DRONE integrates NAV CANADA DroneHub (free) for real-time airspace authorization

**D-DRONE Fleet Registry (D-ASSET extension):**

Each drone registered as a D-ASSET with additional fields:
- Transport Canada registration number
- Assigned pilot (must hold appropriate certificate for the airspace class)
- Battery count and cycle count per battery (drone battery life degrades with cycles)
- Total flight hours (maintenance trigger)
- Last maintenance inspection date
- Insurance coverage status
- Pre-flight checklist per model

**Pre-flight protocol (integrated into D-JOB):**
Before any drone deployment, D-JOB card generates a pre-flight checklist:
- [ ] Airspace check (NAV CANADA DroneHub clearance)
- [ ] Weather check (D-WEATHER: wind < 25 km/h, no precipitation, visibility > 3 miles)
- [ ] Battery charged (>80% minimum)
- [ ] Props inspected (no cracks or chips)
- [ ] Memory card cleared
- [ ] Assigned pilot confirmed (certificate verified in D-TRAIN)
- [ ] Client permission confirmed (for footage that will be shared)

**Battery management:**
Battery is the drone's consumable. D-DRONE tracks:
- Charge cycles per battery (replacement at 200 cycles for DJI standard batteries)
- Storage charge (batteries stored at 40-60% for longevity — D-ASSET flags batteries left at full charge overnight)
- Charging queue (if multiple batteries need charging before a flight day)

**Drone training pathway (in D-TRAIN):**
```
Level 0: Drone awareness (all crew — 1hr online)
Level 1: Basic RPAS Pilot Certificate (Transport Canada exam — any crew member)
Level 2: Advanced RPAS Pilot Certificate (exam + flight review — dedicated pilots)
Level 3: Drone photography & video (internal training — for content quality)
Level 4: Site survey & photogrammetry (3D mapping from drone imagery — advanced)
```

D-Credits awarded at each level. Level 2 pilots earn D-Credits per flight mission.

### 12.2 D-IoT: Machine Intelligence Layer

Every major machine in the cooperative fleet gets a low-cost IoT module that turns it into a data-producing node. This does not require expensive proprietary systems — it uses commodity hardware.

**IoT module per machine (cost: $80–150 CAD assembled):**

| Component | Function | Hardware |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| GPS tracker | Real-time location, geofence | SIM7000G or similar |
| Engine hours counter | Actual operating hours (not calendar) | Pulse counter on ignition circuit |
| Fuel level sensor | Current fuel %, consumption rate | Capacitive or resistive sender |
| LTE module | Data transmission (cellular) | Integrated in GPS module |
| Solar top-up | Battery maintenance | Small solar panel on cab |
| Microcontroller | Local processing, data packaging | ESP32 or Raspberry Pi Zero 2W |

**Data sent to D-FLEET hub every 5 minutes (when engine running):**
- GPS coordinates
- Engine on/off status
- Engine hours (cumulative)
- Fuel level
- Speed (if moving — detects unauthorized use)

**D-IoT alerts:**
| Trigger | Alert |
|---------|-------|
| Machine moves without D-MOVE transport logged | Unauthorized movement alert → owner |
| Machine leaves geofenced site without job closed | Missing asset alert |
| Fuel below 15% | Refuel before next deployment |
| Engine hours hit maintenance interval | Service due alert → D-MAINT |
| Machine idle at job site for >2 hours | Productivity check — is crew blocked? |
| Machine not returned to yard by end of shift | D-MOVE return transport needed |

**GPS real-time map:**
All machine IoT feeds aggregate on the D-FLEET dashboard — owner sees every machine's location, status, and fuel level on a single map. No phone calls to find equipment.

### 12.3 D-UTIL: Asset Utilization Engine

Utilization is the most important metric for capital equipment that no small landscaping company tracks. D-UTIL makes it automatic.

**Utilization rate formula:**
```
Utilization % = (Actual operating hours / Available hours in period) × 100

Available hours = (Working days in period × Daily shift hours) − Maintenance hours
```

**Utilization benchmarks:**
| Rate | Interpretation | Action |
|------|---------------|--------|
| <30% | Severely underutilized | List on D-MARKET for rental; defer replacement; evaluate disposal |
| 30–60% | Underutilized | Optimize dispatch; seek additional work; promote to network |
| 60–80% | Healthy utilization | Monitor; plan for peak demand |
| 80–90% | High utilization | Plan redundancy; evaluate acquisition of second unit |
| >90% | At capacity | Acquire additional unit immediately; risk of failure from overuse |

**Cross-asset scheduling:**
D-UTIL feeds D-ROUTE's job scheduling:
- Jobs that need a specific machine are scheduled when that machine is available
- Multiple jobs requesting the same machine in the same day → D-UTIL scores which is higher priority
- Automatically identifies days when a machine is committed to Site A but requested by Sites B and C → escalates to foreman for dispatch decision

**Buy vs. lease vs. rent decision engine:**
Given real utilization data, D-UTIL calculates:
```
Own cost per operating hour = (Purchase price − Residual value + Total maintenance + Insurance) / Total operating hours

Rent cost per operating hour = (Daily rental rate × days rented) / Operating hours

Lease cost per operating hour = (Monthly lease + Maintenance package) / Operating hours at current utilization
```

When own cost per hour exceeds rent cost per hour at current utilization → flag for acquisition model review.

**Fleet composition analysis (quarterly):**
D-UTIL generates a report:
- Which machines are consistently at capacity (potential acquisition target)
- Which are consistently underutilized (rental or disposal candidates)
- Projected ROI for proposed new equipment based on job pipeline
- Optimal fleet size for next season given D-GROW pipeline

### 12.4 D-RENTAL: Equipment Rental Exchange

Idle equipment costs money. D-RENTAL turns idle hours into revenue.

**Outbound rental (Sod Boys rents to others):**

Any asset with utilization below 50% in the coming week can be listed on D-MARKET:
- Listing includes: machine specs, available dates, daily/weekly rate, pickup or delivery options
- D-ASSET tracks the machine's rental history and generates a utilization/revenue report
- Renter signs a digital liability waiver (cooperative is indemnified)
- D-Credit earned by the crew member who coordinates the rental

**Inbound rental (Sod Boys rents from others):**

When a job requires equipment not in the fleet:
1. D-RENTAL first checks D-MARKET (network cooperative partners)
2. If not available in network, checks commercial rental (Sunbelt, MacAllister, Action Equipment Ottawa)
3. Generates side-by-side cost comparison
4. Rental request submitted through D-JOB, approved by Finance Circle if over threshold

**Homeowner/community rental program:**
Small equipment (aerators, dethatchers, sod cutters) can be rented by D-MEMBER clients:
- Client requests rental through D-CLIENT portal
- Equipment reserved for non-work periods (evenings, weekends)
- Delivery/pickup coordinated through D-MOVE
- Payment processed via D-Credit or standard payment, at cooperative rental rates
- Rental income goes to equipment cooperative fund

**Fleet expansion through rental revenue:**
- Rental revenue tracked as a separate revenue line in the cooperative treasury
- Designated portion (50%) goes to the equipment reserve fund
- When equipment reserve fund hits the acquisition threshold for a new asset: D-UTIL analysis determines if the acquisition is justified, then D-AGORA votes

### 12.5 D-MAINT: Preventive Maintenance Protocol

Reactive maintenance (fix it when it breaks) costs 3–5× more than preventive maintenance and destroys utilization rates. D-MAINT turns maintenance from a surprise into a schedule.

**Maintenance trigger sources:**
| Source | Example | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| IoT engine hours | 250 hours on skid steer | Oil change alert fires |
| Calendar interval | Annual blade inspection | Scheduled regardless of hours |
| Crew report | "Machine pulling left" | Yellow status, inspection within 24h |
| Pre-season audit | Spring startup checklist | Full inspection before season start |
| Post-season audit | Fall shutdown checklist | Winterization protocol |
| Incident report | Machine struck curb | Immediate inspection, downtime logged |

**Maintenance record per asset:**
Every service event logged in D-ASSET:
- Date, engine hours at service
- What was done (oil, filters, belts, blades, hydraulics)
- Who performed the service (internal crew or shop)
- Cost (tracked in D-UTIL)
- Next service due (hours or date, whichever first)
- Parts used (D-SUPPLY records, enables bulk parts purchasing)

**Internal maintenance capability:**
- D-TRAIN includes basic machine maintenance for crew leads
- Simple services (oil changes, filter replacements, blade sharpening) done in-house
- D-Credits earned for maintenance duty
- Specialty services (hydraulics, engine overhaul) sent to certified shop with D-ASSET service record attached

**Winterization protocol (Ottawa-specific):**
November shutdown checklist per machine:
- [ ] Fuel stabilizer added (or tank run empty for non-ethanol systems)
- [ ] Battery disconnected or on trickle charger
- [ ] Hydraulic fluid level checked
- [ ] Greasing of all fittings
- [ ] Blade/cutting element inspection and replacement if needed
- [ ] Stored in covered location, documented in D-ASSET
- [ ] Spring startup checklist created and scheduled

**Parts cooperative:**
Frequently used consumable parts (oil, filters, belts, blades) ordered in bulk through D-SUPPLY. D-MAINT tracks consumption rates and pre-orders before stock drops below two-service threshold.

## Tier 13: D-GROW — Cooperative Growth Engine

*"The D-Central way of acquiring customers is not to buy attention. It is to be so visibly valuable in the community that attention comes to you — and when it does, the network compounds it."*

Advertising spend is a tax the cooperative pays to get in front of strangers. D-GROW builds systems where every completed job, every satisfied member, every crew member's presence in a neighborhood, and every partner relationship generates the next job. The economics invert: growth costs less as the network grows larger.

---

### 13.1 D-LEADS: Inbound Lead Management

Every lead is a potential cooperative community member. D-LEADS manages the pipeline from first contact to D-JOB creation.

**Lead sources tracked:**
| Source | D-LEADS Tag | Credit Assigned To |
|--------|------------|-------------------|
| D-MEMBER referral | `referral` | Referring member earns D-Credits |
| D-CANVAS canvass | `canvas` | Crew member who logged earns D-Credits |
| Organic search | `organic` | D-SEO attribution |
| Social media | `social` | D-CONTENT attribution |
| Partner referral | `partner` | Partner relationship credited |
| Cold contact | `cold` | No credit |
| Repeat client | `repeat` | Auto-creates next job, no lead handling |

**Lead pipeline states:**
```
NEW LEAD
   ↓ (response within 2 hours — tracked)
CONTACT MADE
   ↓
SITE VISIT SCHEDULED
   ↓ (D-QUOTE generated at visit)
QUOTE SENT
   ↓ (follow-up at 48h if no response)
ACCEPTED → D-JOB CREATED → D-MEMBER INVITED
DECLINED → Reason logged → Future follow-up trigger (next spring)
```

**Response time protocol:**
Response within 2 hours wins 50% more landscaping contracts than response after 24 hours (industry data). D-LEADS:
- Sends immediate automated confirmation to the lead ("Got your request — someone from our team will be in touch within 2 hours")
- Pings the on-call crew lead or owner
- Escalates to second contact if no response in 2 hours
- Tracks response time per crew lead — performance visible to D-AGORA

**Lead scoring:**
| Factor | Score Boost |
|--------|------------|
| Referred by D-MEMBER | +30 |
| In an existing neighborhood cluster | +20 |
| Previous interaction (was a lead before) | +15 |
| Large property (high-value potential) | +10 |
| Community organization (HOA, community garden) | +25 |

High-score leads get prioritized response and personal outreach from a crew lead, not just a form response.

### 13.2 D-NEIGHBOR: Neighborhood Network Protocol

The most powerful sales tool a local service company has is a neighbor watching good work happen next door. D-NEIGHBOR systematizes this effect.

**Neighborhood cluster formation:**
- When 3+ D-MEMBER clients exist on the same street or within a 500m radius: that area becomes a **D-NEIGHBOR cluster**
- D-ROUTE automatically schedules cluster jobs together (the crew comes to the street, not one house at a time)
- Visible truck + crew activity → passive marketing to non-member neighbors
- Drone footage from cluster jobs shows the whole street before/after → shared to neighborhood channels with member permission

**New neighbor offer:**
When a D-MEMBER client refers a direct neighbor:
- Neighbor receives a "neighborhood introduction" pricing offer (10% below standard quote)
- Offer framed as: "Your neighbor [first name only] is part of our cooperative community — they thought you might want the same quality"
- Offer expires at end of season (urgency)
- Converting neighbor is immediately invited to D-MEMBER community

**Street completion milestone:**
When a street reaches 60%+ D-MEMBER penetration:
- Street becomes a **Cooperative Street** — named, recognized on the D-CONTENT public map
- All members on the street get a loyalty bonus (D-Credits, priority scheduling)
- Cooperative Streets are marketed as community proof — "Cooperative Street" signs can be physical (small yard signs with member permission)
- Acts as social proof for surrounding streets

**HOA and neighborhood association contracts:**
- When a cluster forms in an HOA-governed community: D-GROW targets the HOA directly
- Block contract: HOA contracts for all common areas + any interested member properties
- Single invoice, single scheduling block, maximum D-ROUTE efficiency
- HOA gets cooperative community pricing + D-CLIENT transparency portal for the board

### 13.3 D-CANVAS: Field Lead Generation

Field crew see more Ottawa lawns and properties in a season than any salesperson could. D-CANVAS turns their presence into a structured lead-generation channel.

**How D-CANVAS works:**
1. Crew member at a job site notices a neighboring property with visible need (dying sod, bare patches, overgrown areas, obvious new construction)
2. Opens D-CANVAS in D-MESH (one-tap access from the main menu)
3. Logs: address (auto-populated by GPS if standing near it), property condition (1-5 photo + note), crew member's observation, permission to contact (if spoken to the owner)
4. Entry syncs to D-LEADS as a `canvas` lead
5. Crew member earns D-Credits per canvas entry that converts to a booked job

**Canvas entry takes 90 seconds.** It requires no special knowledge — "the lawn next door looks dead, they might want sod" is a valid canvas entry.

**Door hanger QR campaign:**
- Printed door hanger cards with cooperative branding and a QR code
- QR links to a landing page with: job photos from that neighborhood, crew story, D-MEMBER offer, quick quote request form
- Cards deployed at neighboring properties while crew is on site (takes 5 minutes, crew earns D-Credits)
- Each door hanger QR is address-tracked (different QR per canvassing run) — D-LEADS knows which doors generated which leads

**Community event presence:**
- Ottawa community events (Glebe Garage Sale, Westboro Street Festival, community garden workdays)
- Cooperative tent or table, crew members representing, D-MEMBER sign-up sheet
- Presence earns brand visibility in target neighborhoods; crew members staffing earn D-Credits

### 13.4 D-CONTENT: Cooperative Content Engine

Every completed Sod Boys job is content. Every crew member story is content. The cooperative identity is content. D-CONTENT makes this systematic without requiring a marketing team.

**Automatic content generation from operations:**

Every job with client permission produces:
- **Before photo** (logged in D-JOB at site arrival)
- **Progress photos** (logged at milestones)
- **After photo** (logged at job completion)
- **Drone footage** (if D-DRONE deployed at that job)
- **Client testimonial prompt** (sent via D-CLIENT after sign-off)

D-CONTENT formats these into:
- Instagram/TikTok post (before/after vertical, 15s)
- Google Business Profile photo update
- Website portfolio gallery entry
- Neighborhood channel post (Nextdoor, Facebook neighborhood groups — with address-level precision)

**Crew stories:**
- Monthly "crew spotlight" feature — one crew member's story, why they joined the cooperative, what they're working toward
- Cooperative ownership story: "At Sod Boys, when we do good work, we all share in it"
- D-TRAIN milestones: crew member passes drone certification → shared as cooperative news

**The cooperative differentiator:**
No competing landscaping company in Ottawa is a worker cooperative. This is a genuine differentiator that a segment of clients will actively seek out and pay for. D-CONTENT amplifies it:
- "We're Ottawa's only worker-owned landscaping cooperative"
- "Our crew aren't just employees — they're co-owners. That's why they care."
- Cooperative principles in all client communications (not performatively — as context for why the work is done differently)

**Content calendar:**
D-CONTENT maintains a weekly posting schedule populated automatically from D-JOB completions and D-TRAIN milestones. Manual posts from the owner or crew leads can be added. The calendar ensures consistent presence without requiring anyone to "remember to post."

**Video content leverage:**

## Tier 4 Expansion: D-TRAIN v2.0

*Full replacement of D-TRAIN section from v1.0.*

D-TRAIN is the cooperative's human capital architecture. It is not an HR system — it is a structured development pathway that turns a seasonal laborer into a cooperative operator.

**Career Progression Framework:**

```
CANDIDATE MEMBER (Season 1)
  Requirements: Onboarding complete, WHMIS certified
  Can: execute assigned work, earn D-Credits, use all D-MESH functions
  Earns: base hourly rate, D-Credits
          ↓ (season completion + 2-member sponsorship)

CREW MEMBER (Full Worker Member)
  Requirements: Season 1 complete, member-sponsored, voted in
  Can: vote on Operations circle decisions, propose changes, mentor candidates
  Earns: + cooperative profit share, equity accumulation
          ↓ (2 seasons + site lead assessment)

SITE LEAD
  Requirements: D-QUOTE certified, D-DRONE Level 1 minimum, first aid certified
  Can: run a site independently, generate quotes, assign daily tasks
  Earns: + site lead stipend per day led
          ↓ (3 seasons + all major certifications + member vote)

COOPERATIVE FOREMAN
  Requirements: Advanced RPAS, D-UTIL proficiency, governance training
  Can: approve quotes under threshold, manage fleet dispatch, represent in partner meetings
  Earns: + foreman stipend, Finance Circle membership
```

**Certification pathways:**

| Certification | Provider | D-Train Level | D-Credits Earned |
|--------------|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| WHMIS 2015 | Online (CCOHS or similar) | Mandatory Day 1 | 0 (required) |
| First Aid Level C + AED | St. John Ambulance Ottawa | Level 1 | 100 |
| Basic RPAS Pilot | Transport Canada online exam | Drone Level 1 | 150 |
| Advanced RPAS Pilot | TC written exam + flight review | Drone Level 2 | 300 |
| Pesticide Applicator (ON) | Ministry of Environment | Level 2 | 200 |
| Equipment Operator Class A (walk-behind) | Internal assessment | Level 1 | 75 |
| Equipment Operator Class B (ride-on) | Internal assessment | Level 2 | 150 |
| Equipment Operator Class C (heavy — skid steer) | Internal + third-party | Level 3 | 250 |
| Site Lead Assessment | Foreman evaluation | Pathway milestone | 500 |
| D-QUOTE Certification | Internal (pricing engine training) | Level 2 | 100 |
| D-DRONE Survey | Internal (photogrammetry) | Drone Level 3 | 200 |
| Cooperative Governance | Internal (D-AGORA training) | Member track | 100 |

**Peer training program:**
- Experienced crew members who train new crew earn D-Credits per training session
- Training quality tracked: if a crew member trained by X consistently fails assessments, X's training credits are reviewed
- Peer trainers document sessions in D-TRAIN (date, trainee, skills covered, assessment outcome)
- This builds institutional knowledge into the cooperative's human capital, not into any individual's head

**D-CAMP: Annual Skills Week:**
One week at season end (October, before winter layoff):
- Intensive skills training for the upcoming season
- Drone flying practice session
- Equipment maintenance workshop
- Cooperative governance training for new members
- Financial literacy session (understanding the cooperative treasury and profit share)
- Team building
- Fully paid (cooperative operating cost, or subsidized through D-Credits)

**Off-season development:**
- Online courses subsidized through D-Credit (cooperative pays up to X credits/month toward approved certifications)
- Algonquin College part-time enrollment support for interested members
- Financial literacy: crew members who want to understand cooperative finances get a dedicated session with the Finance Circle
